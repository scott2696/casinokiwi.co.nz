NZ Online Casino Law 2026: Is Online Gambling Legal in New Zealand?

By Maia Williams, Editor, Bonuses & Regulation Updated 01/09/2026

New Zealand is midway through the biggest change to its gambling law in twenty years. Here is what has actually happened, what happens next, and what any of it means for you as a player.

New Zealand online gambling: the timeline

Who regulates what

Department of Internal Affairs

The gambling regulator. Administers the Gambling Act 2003 and the new online casino licensing regime, licenses Class 4 societies and casino venues, enforces advertising rules, and will supervise the licensed online operators from 2027.

TAB NZ

Statutory body holding exclusive rights to sports and racing betting, operating with Betcha under an Entain arrangement. Returns funding to the racing industry.

Inland Revenue

Does not tax recreational gambling winnings. Does treat cryptocurrency as property, which is where most Kiwi players' tax exposure actually sits. See our tax guide.

Offshore regulators

Curaçao Gaming Control Board, Anjouan Gaming Authority, Tobique Gaming Commission and similar bodies license the operators currently serving New Zealanders. They are real regulators with real licence registers, but they offer materially weaker dispute resolution than the Malta Gaming Authority or the UK Gambling Commission — and none of them answers to a New Zealander.

How to complain about an online casino

There is no New Zealand ombudsman for an offshore casino, so the process runs through the operator and then its regulator. Do it in this order and keep everything in writing.

  1. Raise it with support, in writing. Use email or keep the live chat transcript. State the account name, the dates, the amounts and what you want. Verbal calls leave no record and are close to useless later.
  2. Escalate to a complaints or compliance address. Licensed operators must provide one, usually in the terms and conditions rather than the help centre.
  3. Give them the stated timeframe. Most licences require a substantive response within a set period. Note the date you started.
  4. Take it to the licensing regulator. The Curaçao Gaming Control Board and the Anjouan authority both accept player complaints. You will need the licence number, which is why we tell you to verify it before depositing.
  5. Use an independent mediator. Services such as eCOGRA and the casino complaint databases run by major review portals carry real weight, because operators care about their public record.
  6. Tell us. We de-list operators that stop paying, and a documented case from a reader is exactly the evidence that triggers it. Contact us.
What you cannot do

You will not get a New Zealand court or regulator to compel an offshore operator to pay you, and the Consumer Guarantees Act has little practical reach over a company with no New Zealand presence. That asymmetry is the single strongest argument for choosing operators on verifiable licensing and tested payout behaviour rather than on bonus size.

What actually changes for you when licences land

That last row is worth planning around. In every market that has moved from offshore to licensed — Britain, Sweden, Ontario — headline bonuses shrank once operators came under domestic rules. Better protection, smaller offers, is the usual trade.

The short answer

It has never been an offence for a New Zealand resident to play at an online casino based overseas. The Gambling Act 2003 regulates who may operate remote gambling from within New Zealand; it does not penalise the individual placing the bet. What is changing is the operator side. The Online Casino Gambling Act is creating a licensed domestic market for the first time: the Department of Internal Affairs is issuing up to 15 licences, each valid up to three years with a five-year renewal path and a cap of three per company. A prohibition on advertising unlicensed online casino gambling has already commenced under section 10, and from 1 December 2026 the prohibition on providing online casino gambling without a licence takes effect for operators that have not applied, with penalties reaching NZ$5 million for a company and NZ$300,000 for an individual.

The two laws that matter

The Gambling Act 2003

The Act that governed New Zealand gambling for two decades. It prohibits operating remote interactive gambling from within New Zealand, restricts advertising overseas gambling operators to New Zealanders, and sets the framework for the country's casinos, gaming machines and lotteries. Crucially for players, it contains no offence for a resident who gambles at an overseas site. That gap is why Kiwis have lawfully played at offshore casinos since the internet arrived.

What this means for you as a player

  • Your own play remains lawful. Nothing in the new regime creates an offence for an individual player. That has not changed and is not proposed to change.
  • The list of available sites will shift. Operators that do not obtain a licence face enforcement pressure from December 2026 onward. Some will exit the New Zealand market; some will apply.
  • Licensed operators will offer stronger protections. A DIA licence brings New Zealand-specific harm-minimisation requirements, a domestic complaints pathway, and a regulator you can actually reach — none of which a Curaçao or Anjouan licence provides.
  • Advertising is already restricted. The section 10 prohibition on advertising unlicensed online casino gambling is in force now, not from December.

Sports betting is governed separately

A common confusion. Sports and racing betting does not fall under the Online Casino Gambling Act. Legislation in force from July 2025 gave TAB NZ, with Betcha, exclusive rights to offer sports and racing betting to New Zealanders and barred offshore bookmakers from taking New Zealand business. Two separate regimes with two different sets of rules. See our online betting guide for the betting side.

Other rules Kiwi players should know

  • Minimum age. 18 for online gambling. 20 to enter a physical casino venue in New Zealand.
  • Tax. Recreational gambling winnings are not assessable income and are not subject to GST. See our tax guide.
  • Self-exclusion. New Zealand's domestic venue self-exclusion schemes do not extend to offshore online operators. You must self-exclude with each site individually. See responsible gambling.
  • Consumer protection. The Consumer Guarantees Act and Fair Trading Act have limited practical reach over an operator with no New Zealand presence. Your recourse is the operator's own regulator.

NZ online casino law: FAQ

Is it illegal to play at an online casino in New Zealand?

No. The Gambling Act 2003 restricts operating remote interactive gambling from within New Zealand but contains no offence for a New Zealand resident who plays at an overseas-based online casino. The new Online Casino Gambling Act regulates operators, not players.

When do New Zealand online casino licences take effect?

The Department of Internal Affairs ran an expression of interest process, an auction and a full licence application stage through 2026. From 1 December 2026, the prohibition on providing online casino gambling without a licence takes effect for operators that have not applied. Operators with an application under assessment may continue serving New Zealand players under an exemption, but without advertising.

How many online casino licences will New Zealand issue?

Up to 15. Each licence runs for up to three years with the possibility of a five-year renewal, and no single company may hold more than three licences under this process.

What are the penalties for operating without a licence?

Up to NZ$5 million for a body corporate and up to NZ$300,000 for an individual, from 1 December 2026. Advertising unlicensed online casino gambling is separately prohibited under section 10 of the Act, which has already commenced.

Does the new law cover sports betting?

No. Sports and racing betting is governed separately. Legislation in force from July 2025 gave TAB NZ, alongside Betcha, exclusive rights to offer sports and racing betting to New Zealanders and barred offshore bookmakers from accepting New Zealand business.

What is the penalty for online gambling in New Zealand?

There is none for the player. The Gambling Act 2003 and the Online Casino Gambling Act both create offences for operators, not for individuals. No New Zealander has been penalised for playing at an offshore online casino, and no penalty is proposed.

How do I complain about an online casino as a New Zealander?

Raise it in writing with the operator's support team, then escalate to its complaints or compliance address, then take it to the licensing regulator — the Curaçao Gaming Control Board and the Anjouan authority both accept player complaints, and you will need the licence number. Independent mediators such as eCOGRA also carry weight. There is no New Zealand ombudsman for an offshore operator.

Will bonuses get smaller once NZ licences go live?

Almost certainly. Every market that has moved from offshore to domestic licensing — Britain, Sweden, Ontario — saw headline bonuses shrink as operators came under local rules on advertising and inducements. Stronger player protection with smaller offers is the usual trade.

What happens to my account at an offshore casino after 1 December 2026?

That depends on whether the operator applies for a New Zealand licence. Those with an application under assessment may continue serving New Zealand players under an exemption, without advertising. Those that do not apply face the prohibition on unlicensed provision. Withdraw balances rather than leaving funds sitting at a site whose position is unclear.

Is it legal to advertise online casinos in New Zealand?

Not if the operator is unlicensed. A prohibition on advertising unlicensed online casino gambling has already commenced under section 10 of the Online Casino Gambling Act. Licensed operators will be able to advertise within the rules once the regime is running.
