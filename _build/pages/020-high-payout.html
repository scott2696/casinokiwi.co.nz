How to find the real RTP before you spin

Why the same pokie pays differently at different sites

Published RTP is per game build, not per casino, and operators choose which build to license. Here is how I check, in about fifteen seconds.

  1. Open the game. Load it in demo mode if you can — no deposit needed.
  2. Find the information panel. It is the i icon, the paytable button, or the burger menu inside the game window, depending on the studio.
  3. Scroll to the rules page. The RTP is stated in the game rules, usually at the bottom, usually as "The theoretical return to player is…".
  4. Check for a range. If it says something like "94.00% / 96.50%", the operator has more than one build available and you are being served one of them. A single figure is a better sign.
  5. Compare with the studio's published figure. Pragmatic Play and Play'n GO publish theirs. A gap means the low build.

Payout terms, defined

TermWhat it actually means
RTPReturn to player. The percentage of all money wagered that a game returns over its lifetime. Theoretical, long-run, and never a prediction of your session.
House edge100% minus RTP. The operator's expected share of everything staked.
Volatility / varianceHow returns are distributed. Low pays often and small; high pays rarely and large.
Hit frequencyHow often a spin produces any win at all, typically 20–30%. Separate from RTP.
Max winThe cap on a single game round, expressed as a multiple of stake — 5,000× is common, 50,000× exists.
TurnoverTotal money staked, not money lost. NZ$4,000 of turnover on a 96% game costs about NZ$160.
Game weightingHow much a game counts toward bonus wagering. Pokies usually 100%, table games often 10% or zero.
Payout percentageUsed loosely by casinos to mean a site-wide average. It is not audited or standardised — treat it as marketing, not data.

Do casinos change the RTP of their games?

Casinos cannot alter a certified game build, but studios including Pragmatic Play and Play'n GO ship multiple RTP configurations of the same title, and the operator chooses which to license. A pokie with identical name and artwork can run at 96.5% at one site and 94% at another. Always open the game info panel and read the RTP for that specific lobby.

Does a high payout casino mean I will win?

No. RTP is a long-run average across millions of spins, and short-run variance dominates any individual session. A higher RTP reduces the expected cost of playing over time; it does not make a session profitable. Treat the difference between 94% and 97% as the difference between an expensive and a cheap hobby, not as a route to income.

Do NZ pub pokies pay less than online pokies?

Substantially less. Class 4 gaming machines in New Zealand pubs and clubs have a legal minimum return to player of 85% and typically run in the high 80s to low 90s. Online pokies from studios like Pragmatic Play, NetEnt and Play'n GO publish 95% to 97%. On NZ$100 of turnover that is roughly NZ$10 of expected cost versus NZ$4.

How do I check the RTP of an online pokie?

Open the game, find the information or paytable icon inside the game window, and read the rules page — the theoretical return is stated there. If you see a range such as 94.00% / 96.50%, the studio ships multiple builds and the operator has chosen one. A single stated figure is a better sign.

What is volatility and does it matter more than RTP?

Volatility describes how a game's returns are distributed: low pays small and often, high pays rarely and large. For a single session it matters more than RTP, because it decides whether your bankroll survives long enough to reach a feature. Match volatility to bankroll — high volatility on a small balance usually ends early.

What is a good max win on a pokie?

Most modern pokies cap a single round at 5,000x your stake, with high-volatility titles reaching 10,000x to 50,000x. A high max win almost always signals high volatility, which means longer losing runs. It is a ceiling, not an expectation.

Is a casino's advertised payout percentage trustworthy?

Treat it as marketing. Site-wide payout percentages are not standardised or independently audited the way individual game RTPs are, and the figure shifts with the mix of games people happen to play. The per-game RTP in the info panel is the number that means something.
