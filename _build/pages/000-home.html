Are online casinos legal in New Zealand?

Yes for you as a player, and the law around operators is changing fast. Two pieces of legislation matter.

Under the Gambling Act 2003, it has never been an offence for a New Zealand resident to gamble at an online casino based overseas. The Act restricts who may operate remote gambling from within New Zealand; it does not penalise the individual placing the bet. That is why Kiwis have legally played at offshore sites for two decades.

The Online Casino Gambling Act then created a licensed domestic market for the first time. The Department of Internal Affairs is issuing up to 15 online casino licences, each running up to three years with a five-year renewal path, and no single company may hold more than three. The auction and application process ran through 2026, and from 1 December 2026 the prohibition on providing online casino gambling without a licence takes effect for operators that have not applied. Penalties reach NZ$5 million for a company and NZ$300,000 for an individual.

What this means for you in practice

A prohibition on advertising unlicensed online casino gambling has already commenced under section 10 of the Act. Operators with a licence application under assessment can keep serving New Zealand players under an exemption, but without advertising, until their applications are decided. For players, nothing about the legality of your own play has changed — but the list of sites available to New Zealanders will look different once licensing settles. We track every DIA announcement on our NZ online casino law page and will re-rank this list around licensed operators as they go live.

Two other rules are worth knowing. Betting on sport and racing is separate law: since 2025, TAB NZ (with Betcha) holds the sole legal right to take New Zealand sports and racing bets. And the minimum age is 18 for online play, while entering a physical New Zealand casino venue requires you to be 20.

Casino bonuses and wagering requirements, explained

A welcome bonus is not free money. It is a loan of playing credit with a turnover condition attached, and the condition is where the value lives or dies. Here is how to price any offer in about thirty seconds.

The one calculation that matters

Multiply the bonus amount by the wagering multiple. That is the turnover you must generate before a withdrawal is permitted. Then check what the multiple applies to — "bonus only" is far better than "deposit plus bonus".

  • NZ$600 bonus at 40x (bonus only) = NZ$24,000 of turnover.
  • NZ$600 bonus at 10x (deposit + bonus, on a NZ$100 deposit) = NZ$7,000 of turnover.

The second offer is more than three times easier to clear despite being the same headline size. This is why Smash and Rivo score well on bonus fairness while sites with larger headline numbers do not.

The four clauses that quietly kill an offer

  1. Game weighting. Pokies usually count 100% toward wagering. Blackjack and roulette often count 10% or less, and live dealer games sometimes count zero. If you are a table player, a slots bonus is close to worthless.
  2. Max bet while wagering. Typically NZ$5 to NZ$8 per spin. Exceed it once and the operator can void the bonus and everything won with it.
  3. Expiry. Anywhere from three days to thirty. Lucky Vibe's three-day window is the tightest on this page — you need a plan before you claim it.
  4. Max cashout. Common on no-deposit offers. You might clear NZ$500 of winnings and be allowed to withdraw NZ$100 of it.

Do you pay tax on casino winnings in New Zealand?

No. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational gambling winnings as assessable income, so a pokies win, a blackjack session or a jackpot is yours in full. There is no GST to account for and nothing to declare on an IR3 for ordinary recreational play. New Zealand is unusual in this respect and it is one of the genuine advantages of playing from here.

Two caveats. If gambling is your business rather than your recreation — a professional player with a systematic profit-making intention — different rules apply and you should get advice. And cryptocurrency is treated as property, not currency: if you win in Bitcoin and its value rises before you convert to NZD, that gain can be taxable even though the gambling win itself was not. How to spot an unsafe online casino

Every site on this page cleared our trust checks, but you will meet others. These are the signals that should stop you depositing, in the order we check them.

⚠

No verifiable licence

A licence badge in the footer is not a licence. Find the number, then look it up on the regulator's own register. Roby Casino does not clearly publish its licensing, which is why it sits at 4.0 rather than higher despite having the biggest game library we counted.

🔒

Terms that contradict the ad

A NZ$20,000 headline with a NZ$500 max cashout buried in clause 14 is not a NZ$20,000 offer. If the terms page and the banner disagree, believe the terms page.

🕑

KYC only after you win

Legitimate operators verify identity at sign-up or first withdrawal. Sites that never mention it until you request a payout, then demand documents repeatedly, are managing withdrawals rather than managing risk.

🚫

Missing or buried RG tools

Deposit limits, loss limits, reality checks, time-outs and self-exclusion should be reachable in three clicks from your account page. If they are hidden, the operator is not built around player welfare.
