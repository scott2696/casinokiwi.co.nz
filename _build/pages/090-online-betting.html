The short answer

Online betting is legal for New Zealanders, but only one operator is licensed to take your bet. Legislation in force from July 2025 gave TAB NZ, with Betcha, exclusive rights to offer sports and racing betting to New Zealand residents and barred offshore bookmakers from accepting New Zealand business. Is online betting legal in New Zealand?

Yes for you. The nuance is on the operator side, and it changed recently enough that most guides still get it wrong.

The current position, September 2026

TAB NZ — operating with Betcha under an Entain arrangement — holds the exclusive legal right to offer sports and racing betting to New Zealanders, under legislation in force from July 2025 that also barred offshore bookmakers from taking New Zealand business. Enforcement targets operators. There is no offence for an individual New Zealand resident placing a bet, and no punter has been penalised. The practical consequence for you is recourse: a dispute with TAB runs through a New Zealand process, while a dispute with an offshore book runs through whichever foreign regulator licensed it.

Do not confuse this with the casino regime. The Online Casino Gambling Act created a licensed domestic market for online casino gambling, with the Department of Internal Affairs issuing up to 15 licences and unlicensed operation prohibited from 1 December 2026. Sports and racing betting is governed separately, under the TAB framework. Two regimes, two sets of rules, frequently mixed up.

Other rules worth knowing: you must be 18 or over to bet online in New Zealand; every operator must verify your identity before paying out; and recreational winnings are not taxed.

How to read decimal odds

New Zealand and Australia use decimal odds, which are the easiest format there is. The number is your total return per dollar staked, including your stake.

Decimal odds converted to returns, implied probability and other formats.
Decimal oddsNZ$10 stake returnsProfitImplied probabilityFractional
1.20NZ$12.00NZ$2.0083.3%1/5
1.50NZ$15.00NZ$5.0066.7%1/2
1.91NZ$19.10NZ$9.1052.4%10/11
2.00NZ$20.00NZ$10.0050.0%Evens
3.50NZ$35.00NZ$25.0028.6%5/2
10.00NZ$100.00NZ$90.0010.0%9/1

Implied probability is 1 divided by the decimal odds. At 2.00 the book is saying the outcome happens half the time. Add the implied probabilities of every outcome in a market and you will get more than 100% — that excess is the bookmaker's margin, or "overround". A two-way market priced 1.91/1.91 carries about 4.7% margin. The same market at 1.95/1.95 carries about 2.6%. Over hundreds of bets, that difference matters far more than any single winner.

The habit that separates winning punters from losing ones

Compare the price on the same selection across two or three books before you stake. A consistent 2–3% better price is worth more over a season than any tipping service, because it compounds on every single bet you place.

Bet types and markets explained

The basics

  • Head to head (moneyline). Pick the winner. Simplest market, tightest margins, best place to start.
  • Line / handicap. One team starts with a virtual points advantage. Turns a lopsided contest into a roughly even-money bet. The standard rugby and league market.
  • Total (over/under). Bet on combined points, runs or goals landing above or below a set number. Independent of who wins.
  • Each way. Racing staple. Two bets in one — half your stake on the win, half on a place. You pay double for the ticket, and get paid on a placing at a fraction of the win odds.

Beyond the basics

  • Multi / accumulator. Several selections combined; every leg must win. The odds multiply, and so does the bookmaker's margin on every leg — a four-leg multi can carry 15%+ built-in margin. Fun, rarely profitable.
  • Bet builder / same-game multi. Combine markets within one fixture. Correlated legs are priced conservatively, so the value is usually worse than it looks.
  • Player props. First try scorer, most tackles, runs scored. The softest markets at most books because they are harder to price. This is where a punter with genuine sport knowledge has the biggest edge.
  • Futures. Season-long markets like NPC winner or Super Rugby champion. Money is tied up for months, so treat it as an entertainment position, not a trade.
  • In-play. Betting during the match, with odds updating live. The best in-play tools include cash-out, which lets you take a guaranteed return before the event finishes — usually at a price that slightly favours the book.

Free bets and betting bonuses: what they are worth

Sports bonuses are structured differently from casino bonuses and the traps are different too.

  • Stake not returned. The most important clause. A NZ$50 free bet at 2.00 returns NZ$50 profit, not NZ$100 — the stake is deducted. That halves the headline value immediately.
  • Minimum odds. Usually 1.50 or higher, so you cannot clear the bonus by backing overwhelming favourites.
  • Turnover requirement. Sports bonuses use lower multiples than casino bonuses. Smash's 15x and Kingdom's 15x on the Tuesday Boost are well below the 30x–40x norm.
  • Excluded markets. Cash-out bets, void legs and some in-play markets often do not count toward turnover.
  • Expiry. Free bets commonly expire in seven days. If you do not bet weekly, much of the value evaporates.

Bankroll and staking: the part that actually decides your year

You can pick winners at a perfectly respectable rate and still lose everything through poor staking. Three rules cover most of it.

  1. Set a bankroll you can afford to lose entirely, separate from your everyday account. This is your entertainment budget for the season, not a float.
  2. Stake 1–2% of that bankroll per bet, flat, regardless of how confident you feel. At 1% you survive a twenty-bet losing run with 80% of your bankroll intact. At 10% you are broke.
  3. Never chase. Doubling the stake after a loss to "get it back" is the single most reliable way to turn a manageable losing week into a serious problem. If you find yourself doing it, stop for the day — and use the site's deposit limit tool before your next session.
A number worth sitting with

Even a genuinely skilled punter betting into a 4% margin needs to win around 54% of even-money bets just to break even. Most people who believe they are ahead are not counting properly. Keep a record of every bet — date, selection, odds, stake, result — in a spreadsheet. It is the single most sobering and most useful thing you can do.

Betting on mobile in New Zealand

Most Kiwi betting now happens on a phone, usually in the last few minutes before a match. TAB offers native iOS and Android apps through the app stores. International sportsbooks generally cannot, because Apple and Google restrict real-money gambling apps in New Zealand — so they build responsive web apps instead. Open the site in Safari or Chrome and use "Add to Home Screen" for a full-screen experience with no app store account required.

Two practical notes: turn on push or email notifications for bet settlement so you are not refreshing a page, and never place an in-play bet on a patchy connection — the odds you tapped may not be the odds you get, and most books reserve the right to reprice or void.

Do you pay tax on betting winnings in New Zealand?

No. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational gambling winnings as assessable income, so sports and racing winnings are yours in full — no income tax, no GST, nothing to declare on an IR3 for ordinary recreational punting. New Zealand is unusually generous here compared with many jurisdictions.

Two exceptions. If betting is carried on as a business with a systematic profit-making intention, different rules apply and you should get professional advice. And if you bet in cryptocurrency, remember that the IRD treats crypto as property: the gambling win is not taxable, but a gain in the coin's value before you convert to NZD can be. Staying safe when you bet online

🔑

Verify before you bet

Upload ID and proof of address on day one. It removes the most common cause of a delayed first withdrawal and confirms the operator's process is legitimate before you have money at stake.

💰

Set a deposit limit immediately

Every reputable book offers daily, weekly and monthly deposit limits. Set one before your first bet, when you are thinking clearly. Increases usually take effect after a cooling-off period; decreases apply instantly.

📊

Track every bet

A simple spreadsheet with date, selection, odds, stake and result. Most punters who think they are ahead are not, and the record is the only honest referee.

⛔

Know the warning signs

Betting more than planned, chasing losses, borrowing to bet, hiding it from whānau, or betting to escape stress. If two or more sound familiar, call the Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655 — free, confidential, 24/7.

Online betting NZ: frequently asked questions

Is online betting legal in New Zealand?

Yes for punters. TAB NZ, alongside Betcha, holds the only legal right to offer sports and racing betting to New Zealand residents under legislation in force from July 2025, and offshore bookmakers are barred from accepting New Zealand business. Enforcement targets operators rather than individuals, and no New Zealand punter has been penalised for placing a bet with an international book, but a dispute with an offshore operator falls outside New Zealand's complaints framework.

What odds format does New Zealand use?

Decimal odds. The number shown is your total return per dollar staked including the stake, so a NZ$10 bet at 2.50 returns NZ$25 for a NZ$15 profit. Implied probability is one divided by the decimal odds, so 2.00 means the bookmaker rates the outcome at 50%.

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in NZ?

No. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational gambling winnings as assessable income, so there is no income tax and no GST on sports or racing winnings. The exceptions are betting carried on as a business, and cryptocurrency, which the IRD treats as property so gains in coin value before conversion to NZD can be taxable.

What is the minimum age for online betting in New Zealand?

You must be 18 or over to bet online in New Zealand. Every operator is required to verify your age and identity before releasing a withdrawal, and accounts opened with false details are closed with winnings forfeited.

How much should I bet per selection?

One to two percent of your total bankroll per bet, staked flat regardless of confidence. At one percent you survive a twenty-bet losing run with eighty percent of your bankroll intact; at ten percent per bet the same run wipes you out. Set the bankroll as an amount you can afford to lose entirely.

What is the fastest way to withdraw betting winnings in NZ?

Cryptocurrency, at ten minutes to four hours once your account is verified. E-wallets such as Skrill and Neteller take two to twenty-four hours, and card or NZD bank transfers take one to five business days. Completing identity verification before you deposit removes the biggest single source of delay.

Can I use POLi to fund a betting account?

Rarely, and it should not be your plan. POLi is deposit-only so it can never be a withdrawal route, and the major New Zealand banks now commonly decline POLi-to-gambling transactions because sharing internet-banking credentials with a third party breaches their terms. Use a debit card, NZD bank transfer or crypto instead.

Are free bets actually free?

They are free to claim but worth less than the headline. In almost all cases the stake is not returned, so a NZ$50 free bet at odds of 2.00 pays NZ$50 profit rather than NZ$100. Most also carry minimum odds of around 1.50, a turnover requirement, excluded markets and a seven-day expiry.

Which sports have the best betting value in New Zealand?

Player prop markets across rugby, league and cricket, because they are harder for bookmakers to price and reward genuine sport knowledge. New Zealand racing is best served by TAB, which prices every domestic meeting with full fields. Main-line football markets carry the tightest margins but also the least pricing error.

What is overround and why does it matter?

Overround is the bookmaker's built-in margin. Add the implied probabilities of every outcome in a market and the total exceeds 100 percent; the excess is the margin. A two-way market priced at 1.91 each side carries about 4.7 percent, while 1.95 each side carries about 2.6 percent. Consistently taking the better price is worth more over a season than any tipping service.

The bottom line

Online betting in New Zealand rewards boring discipline far more than clever selections. Know that TAB NZ is the only licensed operator and what that means for your recourse. Bet in New Zealand dollars so you are not paying a currency spread twice. Compare the price on every selection before you stake. Keep every bet to one or two percent of a bankroll you have decided you can lose. Record everything. And use the deposit limit tools on day one, while it is still an abstract decision.

Do those five things and you will be ahead of the large majority of punters — not necessarily in profit, because the margin is real, but in control, which is the part that actually matters.
