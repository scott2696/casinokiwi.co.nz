How a live casino actually works

Worth understanding, because it explains both why live games are harder to rig and why they behave differently from pokies.

The studio

A physical room — Evolution's are in Latvia, Malta, Georgia and Canada — with real tables, real dealers and multiple cameras. The dealer is an employee of the game provider, not of the casino you signed up with. Dozens of casinos stream the same table simultaneously, which is why you will see identical dealers across different brands.

Optical character recognition

Cameras read the cards and the wheel in real time and convert them to data. That data drives the betting overlay on your screen and settles every bet automatically. It is also why results are auditable after the fact: every round has a video record and a data record that must agree.

The betting window

Each round opens a fixed window — typically 12 to 20 seconds — during which all seated players place bets. When it closes, the dealer acts. You are not playing against an algorithm deciding your outcome; you are watching a physical event and betting before it happens.

Bet behind and unlimited seats

Blackjack tables have seven seats. When they are full, "bet behind" lets you back another player's hand — you get their outcome without their decisions, which is fine if they play basic strategy and expensive if they do not. Infinite Blackjack removes seats altogether and gives everyone the same dealt hand.

The rules card: where live casinos quietly take more

Two tables can look identical and cost very different amounts. This is what I check before sitting down.

A 6:5 blackjack table at 99.5% theoretical becomes roughly 98.1%. That is nearly triple the house edge for a game that looks exactly the same on screen. The rules card is in the corner of the stream or behind the i icon — read it first.

The game shows, and what they really return

Evolution's game shows are what most Kiwis actually open, so they deserve honest numbers rather than enthusiasm.

The side-bet trap

Live blackjack returns 99.5% on the main hand. The side bets beside it — Perfect Pairs, 21+3, Any Pair, Bust It — typically return 90–95%. Betting NZ$5 on the hand and NZ$5 on a side bet roughly halves the quality of your table. If you take one thing from this page: play the hand, skip the side bets.

Practical things that make live play better

  1. Play on wi-fi. Live dealer is a continuous video stream. Drop out mid-hand and most operators play your hand automatically on default strategy — you lose control of a decision you were making.
  2. Learn basic strategy before you sit. It is a chart, not a talent. Playing blackjack without it costs roughly 2% on top of the house edge, turning the best game in the lobby into a mediocre one.
  3. Check the table minimum before you join, not after. Live minimums are higher than pokies; on a bankroll under NZ$100 look specifically for the NZ$1 blackjack and NZ$0.50 roulette tables.
  4. Use the chat sparingly. Dealers are staff being recorded. Abuse gets you removed from the table by the provider, across every casino streaming it.
  5. Do not chase on a fixed-window game. Live rounds run on a clock rather than at your pace, which makes losses accumulate faster than they do on pokies. Set a session limit before you sit.
