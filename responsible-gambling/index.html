Responsible Gambling in New Zealand: Help, Limits and Self-Exclusion

By Maia Williams, Editor, Bonuses & Regulation Updated 01/09/2026

Gambling is entertainment that costs money. For most people that is all it is. For roughly one in five New Zealanders who gamble regularly, it causes some level of harm — and help here is free, confidential and available right now.

Get help now

Gambling Helpline New Zealand — free, confidential, 24 hours a day: 0800 654 655, or free text 8006. You do not need to be in crisis to call, and you do not have to give your name. They also support family and whānau of someone who is gambling. In an emergency, call 111.

Free support services in New Zealand

Recognising gambling harm early

Harm is a spectrum, not a switch. Most people who experience it are not in crisis — they are quietly spending more than they intended, more often than they meant to. These are the signals worth taking seriously.

  • Money signals
    • Spending more than you planned, regularly
    • Chasing losses to "get back to even"
    • Borrowing, or using credit, to gamble
    • Gambling money set aside for rent, kai or bills
    • Not knowing how much you have actually lost
  • Time and behaviour signals
    • Sessions running much longer than intended
    • Gambling to escape stress, boredom or low mood
    • Hiding it from partner, whānau or friends
    • Restlessness or irritability when you cannot play
    • Missing work, study or family commitments

If two or more of those sound familiar, that is worth a phone call. Calling the Helpline does not commit you to anything — most conversations are short, practical and anonymous.

Self-exclusion does not carry across offshore sites

This is important and rarely explained. New Zealand's venue-based self-exclusion schemes cover physical venues here. There is no single register that blocks you from every offshore online casino at once — you must self-exclude with each operator individually. If you are self-excluding, do it at every site you hold an account with, on the same day, and pair it with a blocking tool so a new account is not one search away.

Blocking tools worth using

  • Bank-level gambling blocks. Several New Zealand banks let you block gambling merchant categories on your cards from within the banking app. It is free, immediate, and usually has a delay before it can be lifted. This is the single most effective practical step available to New Zealanders.
  • Gamban and BetBlocker. Software that blocks thousands of gambling sites and apps across your devices. BetBlocker is free.
  • Device-level content filters. iOS Screen Time and Android Digital Wellbeing can restrict categories and apps, ideally with a passcode held by someone you trust.

Setting yourself up before you play

  1. Decide your budget for the month, as an amount you would be genuinely fine losing entirely. That is the price of the entertainment.
  2. Set the deposit limit to that number on day one, before you have played and before it feels restrictive.
  3. Turn on a 30-minute reality check.
  4. Never deposit while playing. If you top up mid-session, you have already left the plan.
  5. Keep gambling money separate from the account that pays rent and bills.
  6. Track what you spend. Most people who think they are roughly even are not.

Supporting someone else

You do not need the person to be ready to stop before you get help yourself. The Gambling Helpline and the Problem Gambling Foundation both support family, whānau and friends directly, free of charge. Practical steps that help: talk when neither of you is angry, focus on what you have noticed rather than accusations, protect shared finances without secrecy, and get your own support. Ultimatums rarely work; steady, non-judgemental presence often does.

Responsible gambling: FAQ

Where can I get free gambling help in New Zealand?

The Gambling Helpline is free, confidential and available 24 hours a day on 0800 654 655, or free text 8006. The Problem Gambling Foundation offers free counselling nationwide on 0800 664 262. Both support family and whānau as well as the person gambling, and you do not need a referral or to give your name.

How do I self-exclude from an online casino?

Find the responsible gambling or account settings section of the site and choose self-exclusion, selecting a period from six months to permanent. It is irreversible for the chosen period at most operators. Because there is no single register covering offshore sites, you must self-exclude with each operator separately, and pairing it with a bank-level gambling block is far more effective.

Can my bank block gambling transactions in NZ?

Yes. Several New Zealand banks let you block gambling merchant category codes on your cards from within the banking app, free and effective immediately, usually with a delay before the block can be lifted. It is the single most effective practical step available to New Zealanders who want to stop.

What is a deposit limit and how does it work?

A deposit limit caps how much you can put into an account over a day, week or month. Set it before your first deposit while the decision is still abstract. Reductions take effect immediately, while increases are usually subject to a cooling-off period of 24 hours or more, which is deliberate.

How do I know if I have a gambling problem?

Warning signs include spending more than planned, chasing losses, borrowing to gamble, using money meant for rent or bills, hiding it from whānau, gambling to escape stress, and feeling restless when you cannot play. If two or more apply, call the Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655 — the conversation is free, anonymous and commits you to nothing.
