Gambling Winnings Tax NZ: Do You Pay Tax on Casino Wins?

By Maia Williams, Editor, Bonuses & Regulation Updated 01/09/2026

What to actually record

For pure NZD play, nothing is required — there is no return to file and no declaration to make. The moment cryptocurrency enters the picture, records matter. This is the whole system, and it fits in a spreadsheet.

Most exchanges will export a CSV containing most of this. Ten minutes a month keeps it current; a year of reconstruction from memory does not work.

Two worked examples

Playing in New Zealand dollars

You deposit NZ$200 by debit card, win NZ$3,000 on a pokie, and withdraw NZ$3,000 to your bank. Nothing is taxable. The win is not assessable income, there is no GST, and there is nothing to declare. Interest the money subsequently earns in your bank account is ordinary investment income and is taxable in the normal way.

Playing in cryptocurrency

You buy NZ$500 of Bitcoin, deposit it, win, and withdraw 0.06 BTC worth NZ$6,000 on the day it lands. Three months later you convert it to New Zealand dollars and it is now worth NZ$7,200.

  • The gambling win — NZ$6,000 — is not taxable.
  • The NZ$1,200 increase between winning the Bitcoin and converting it is a gain on property, and depending on your circumstances and your intention when you acquired it, that can be taxable.

Two separate events, assessed separately. The gambling side is generous; the crypto side follows ordinary property rules. Converting promptly, or using a stablecoin, largely removes the second question.

When does gambling become a business?

There is no threshold, no number of hours and no amount that flips the switch. Inland Revenue would look at the whole picture:

  • Is there a systematic profit-making intention, or is this recreation that sometimes wins?
  • Scale and regularity — occasional play versus a daily occupation.
  • Does skill predominate over chance? Poker and advantage play sit differently from pokies, where no skill can overcome the house edge.
  • Is it your main source of income?
  • Is it run in a business-like way — records, bankroll management, a staking plan?

A recreational player having an exceptional year is not running a business. A professional treating it as an occupation may be. If you think you might be in the second group, take advice from a chartered accountant rather than working it out from a web page — including this one.

Why New Zealand does not tax gambling winnings

New Zealand's income tax system taxes income, and a windfall from a game of chance is not income in the ordinary sense — there is no service provided, no business carried on and no expectation of a recurring return. The same logic exempts lottery prizes and Lotto wins. This is genuinely more generous than many jurisdictions, and it applies whether you win at a New Zealand venue, at TAB, or at an overseas online casino.

The crypto exception, explained properly

Two separate events, assessed separately

Inland Revenue treats cryptocurrency as property. So when you win in crypto, two things happen. First, a gambling win — not taxable. Second, you acquire property. If you later dispose of that property (by converting to NZD, spending it, or swapping it for another coin) and it has risen in value, that gain can be taxable depending on your circumstances and your intention when you acquired it. A NZ$5,000 Bitcoin win that becomes NZ$6,500 before you cash out involves a non-taxable NZ$5,000 gambling win and a potentially taxable NZ$1,500 property gain.

What to actually do about it

  1. Record the NZD value at every point. Date and NZD value of each deposit, each withdrawal and each conversion. Exchange records plus a simple spreadsheet is enough.
  2. Convert promptly if you want simplicity. The shorter the gap between winning crypto and converting to NZD, the smaller any gain to account for.
  3. Use a stablecoin. USDT barely moves against the US dollar, which largely removes the gain question while keeping crypto's speed. See our crypto casinos guide.
  4. Get advice if the amounts are meaningful. Crypto tax in New Zealand turns on intention and pattern of activity, which is fact-specific. An accountant is cheaper than getting it wrong.

When does gambling become a "business"?

There is no bright-line rule and no minimum threshold. Inland Revenue would look at the whole picture: whether there is a systematic profit-making intention, the scale and regularity of activity, whether skill rather than chance predominates, whether it is your main income source, and whether you operate it in a business-like way with records and staking plans. A recreational player who has a very good year is not running a business. A professional poker or advantage player treating it as an occupation may be. If you think you might be in the second category, take advice rather than assuming.

General information, not tax advice

This page explains the general New Zealand position and is not a substitute for advice on your circumstances. For the authoritative position see Inland Revenue, and for anything material to your finances, talk to a chartered accountant.

Gambling winnings tax NZ: FAQ

Do you pay tax on gambling winnings in New Zealand?

No. Inland Revenue does not treat recreational gambling winnings as assessable income, so casino wins, pokies wins, jackpots and winning bets are not subject to income tax or GST and do not need to be declared on an IR3. The exceptions are gambling carried on as a business and cryptocurrency gains.

Do I pay GST on casino winnings in NZ?

No. GST applies to supplies of goods and services in the course of a taxable activity. A recreational gambling win is a windfall, not a supply you have made, so no GST arises and there is nothing to return.

Do I pay tax on crypto casino winnings?

The gambling win itself is not taxable, but Inland Revenue treats cryptocurrency as property rather than currency. If the coin rises in value between winning it and converting it to New Zealand dollars, that gain can be taxable depending on your circumstances and intention. Record the NZD value of every transaction on the date it occurred.

Do I need to declare online casino winnings to the IRD?

Not for recreational play. Winnings are not assessable income so there is nothing to include on your return. Interest earned once the money is in a bank account is ordinary investment income and is taxable, as is any income you earn from gambling-related business activity such as affiliate marketing or streaming.

Is professional gambling taxed in New Zealand?

It can be. There is no fixed threshold; Inland Revenue looks at whether there is a systematic profit-making intention, the scale and regularity of the activity, whether skill predominates over chance, and whether it is operated in a business-like way. A recreational player having a good year is not running a business, but a professional treating it as an occupation may be. Take advice if this could apply to you.

What records should I keep for crypto casino play?

One row per transaction with five fields: date, type (buy, deposit, withdrawal, conversion, sale), coin and amount, the New Zealand dollar value on that date, and a note identifying the exchange, casino or wallet. The NZD value on the day is the critical field — reconstructing it a year later is painful and most exchanges will export a CSV containing most of it.

Can I claim gambling losses against tax in New Zealand?

No. Because recreational winnings are not assessable income, losses are not deductible expenses. You cannot offset a losing year against other income and there is no mechanism to claim anything back. The exemption is generous in one direction only.

Are winnings from a casino overseas taxable in New Zealand?

Not in New Zealand — the same windfall treatment applies regardless of where you won. The country you won in may have withheld tax at source, which is a matter for that jurisdiction. If the amount is significant, take advice on whether any double-tax arrangement applies.

Does a big win affect a benefit or Working for Families?

It can. A lump sum may affect asset or income testing for some entitlements even though it is not taxable income. Check with the Ministry of Social Development or Inland Revenue before you commit the money.

Do I have to tell the IRD about a large gambling win?

Not for recreational play — there is nothing to declare because the win is not assessable income. Your bank may make an anti-money-laundering enquiry about a large deposit, which is a separate process and is satisfied by showing where the funds came from. Keep the casino's withdrawal confirmation.
